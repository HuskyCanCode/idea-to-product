# PDF slide format

Read this when the user requests or accepts a PDF slide deck of the completed report. Reuse the confirmed brief, evidence, and estimates; do not restart intake. The slide offer and opt-in rules remain in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md#5-offer-optional-pdf-slides).

## Default sequence

Use nine main slides followed by a resources appendix. Preserve this order unless the user requests otherwise; split dense slides or combine sparse ones when that improves readability.

| Slide | Reader's question | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Idea and estimated investment | What am I building, and what might it cost? | One-sentence idea, recommendation, development cost range, monthly operating costs, timeline, and the assumptions defining the estimate. |
| 2. Business-model map | How does the business work? | Users, value delivered, required inputs, who pays, payment timing, and where money is spent. Use labeled arrows to distinguish information, service, and payment flows. |
| 3. Idea evaluation | How strong is the opportunity? | Relevant 0–10 scores, evidence, confidence, strengths, and gaps. Keep unsupported dimensions marked “Not assessed”; do not invent an overall score. |
| 4. First version | What should we build first? | Core features, a simple user journey, and features reserved for later. Preserve the user's agreed scope and identify any recommended staging. |
| 5. Validation and delivery plan | How do we turn it into reality? | Research, prototype, pilot, and launch milestones with owners, effort, dependencies, and evidence needed to proceed. Adjust stages to the actual project. |
| 6. Financial scenarios | What would make it sustainable? | Cost breakdown, pricing assumptions, revenue scenarios, acquisition costs when known, and break-even where achievable. Distinguish development investment, recurring costs, revenue, and profit. |
| 7. Legal research findings | What needs checking before launch? | Relevant jurisdiction, sourced findings, unresolved questions, affected activities, and specific next steps. Distinguish verified rules from their tentative application to the product. |
| 8. Ownership, permissions, and credits | What can we use commercially? | Known code, data, images, documents, and other materials; permission status, license obligations, required credits, and practical alternatives. Credit alone does not establish permission. |
| 9. Prioritized next actions | What should I do next? | The most important actions, owners, completion criteria, and remaining decisions. Include effort and dependencies when useful for execution. |
| Resources appendix | Where did the conclusions come from? | Clickable sources, dates, what each supports, detailed calculations, and the full rights register. Add pages as needed rather than shrinking the text. |

## Evidence and presentation

- Put any legal or data-permission issue that could substantially change feasibility or cost on slide 1 as well as in the detailed review. A headline estimate must remain understandable alongside its scope, exclusions, and uncertainty.
- Use landscape slides, one main message per slide, readable charts, and short explanations. Prefer a visual flow on the business-model slide, a compact scorecard, and a milestone sequence for the plan.
- Preserve the report's current scope, unknowns, confidence, material risks, and cost assumptions. Summarizing does not justify adding unsupported claims or implying legal clearance.
- Keep concise citations visible beside material claims and link them to the full resources appendix. Speaker notes alone are insufficient because they may not appear in the PDF.
- Include all relevant resources actually reviewed or relied upon. For each, give its title, publisher/provider, link, date or version where available, access date, and purpose. Label useful but unverified leads separately; do not present search results as reviewed evidence. Preserve required attribution text.
- Use the available presentations and PDF skills for production. Render and inspect every exported page for readable text, complete diagrams, unclipped content, and visible citations. Verify that source links survive export before sharing the downloadable PDF.
