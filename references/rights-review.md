# Rights, attribution, and commercial readiness

Use this as a review method, not a fixed legal opinion. Investigate the user's actual use, jurisdiction, and materials. Recheck current primary sources when making material legal or license claims; a saved URL is a starting point, not proof of present terms.

## Review method

1. Inventory external inputs and contributions: code/snippets/dependencies, fonts, images, icons, text, designs, audio, datasets, listings/reviews, APIs, models, user uploads, contractor and employee work.
2. Establish intended use: internal prototype, hosted service, browser/mobile distribution, resale, modification, redistribution, training, retrieval, or publication. Check the particular version and file/asset scope, not just a repository badge.
3. Inspect the actual license, contract, provider terms, or permission evidence. Record effective/version dates and the date checked. Inspect direct and material transitive software dependencies when a codebase exists.
4. Identify concrete obligations and what triggers them. Distinguish permission, ownership, attribution, and other compliance requirements.
5. Give each unresolved matter an owner and next action. Continue independent work with suitable original, synthetic, or properly authorized materials.

## Keep these questions separate

- **Idea versus expression:** investigate rights in the actual code, text, imagery, or design being reused; do not assume the general idea is copyrighted. Patent, trademark, contract, confidentiality, and other rights can be separate issues. Do not claim a patent or trademark search establishes clearance.
- **Public access versus permission:** a public repository, website, or downloadable file does not alone demonstrate commercial reuse rights. Missing permission is an unknown to resolve, not evidence that credit will cure it.
- **Open source versus source available:** inspect exact conditions. Commercial use can be allowed while redistribution or other obligations still apply. Do not label all copyleft code incompatible with business; evaluate combination, modification, distribution, and network-use triggers under the actual license.
- **Media licenses versus software licenses:** do not automatically apply a Creative Commons license to software. For licensed media check version, attribution, change notices, share-alike, noncommercial, and no-derivatives terms as relevant. Avoid treating summaries or license labels as the complete terms.
- **Facts versus collections and assets:** assess facts, database rights, photographs, descriptions, access terms, and privacy separately. Do not equate a contractual scraping restriction with a finding of criminal conduct. Check authorized APIs or licensed feeds without assuming approval, coverage, storage, or redistribution rights.
- **Credit versus permission:** preserve required copyright/license/NOTICE material in the appropriate locations. Maintain voluntary acknowledgments separately. Never invent a creator, rights holder, permission, endorsement, or license text. A credits page is not a substitute for other obligations.
- **Contribution versus ownership:** inspect employment, contractor, collaborator, and contributor arrangements. Do not assume payment or possession of files establishes every necessary right, or choose a project license without checking ownership and dependency compatibility.
- **AI origin versus rights:** inspect provider/model terms, input permissions, and intended output use. AI generation or rewriting does not guarantee originality, ownership, or noninfringement. Do not reuse private uploads for training or publication merely because the user supplied them for analysis.
- **Product label versus actual activity:** investigate what the service does. Labels such as “education,” “advertising,” or “subscription” do not settle professional licensing or sector rules. Equally, do not assume every analytical product needs a professional license. Verify local rules and any actual exemptions.

## Proportionate adjacent checks

Review these only when implicated: personal data and sensitive uploads, minors, marketing and endorsements, brand confusion, payment renewals/cancellation, consumer claims, regulated advice, accessibility, and platform/provider restrictions. For uploaded documents, map who can access them, vendor processing, necessary fields, retention/deletion, and permitted uses. Recommend controls tied to those flows.

State the applicable jurisdiction and limits of the review. Separate verified requirements, reasonable product recommendations, and questions for a qualified professional. A disclaimer is not a substitute for reliable behavior or meeting applicable requirements.

## Source and rights register

For each material item keep these fields; use an expandable record or two tables rather than an unreadably wide table:

- Item and version; product feature/use.
- Source URL or user-provided document; author/rightsholder if established.
- Intended commercial use and distribution/access mode.
- Exact license/terms/permission evidence, effective date if known, and verification date.
- Obligations: required credit, notices, modification/source obligations, usage/storage limits, and relevant restrictions.
- Planned notice/credit location and source of the exact wording.
- Status: **evidence found—obligations pending**, **obligations verified for stated use**, **permission unresolved**, **replace/exclude**, or **specialist question**.
- Next action, owner, and evidence needed to close it.

Do not claim “obligations verified” after merely finding a license. Unknown entries remain visible. The register documents the checked scope; it is not blanket clearance.

## Useful primary starting points

These are source-discovery pointers, not a globally applicable legal checklist. Open and verify relevant pages when used.

- U.S. copyright subject matter: https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html
- U.S. permission guidance: https://copyright.gov/circs/circ16a.pdf
- U.S. AI copyright work: https://www.copyright.gov/ai/
- Official OSI license texts: https://opensource.org/licenses
- MIT license text: https://opensource.org/license/mit
- Creative Commons guidance and links to legal terms: https://creativecommons.org/faq/

For other jurisdictions and specific products, use the responsible regulator, legislation, licensor, or provider's current primary documents. Verify effective dates, amendments, withdrawals, and applicability; a recently crawled page may still describe superseded guidance.
