# Scoring and the final report

## Score the evidence and plan, not the founder

Use whole-number ratings from 0 to 10 for assessable dimensions. State whether a rating measures potential, plan quality, observed performance, or readiness. Do not mix these into an unexplained overall number.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Demonstrated critical weakness or an explicitly incomplete plan for this dimension |
| 3–4 | Some foundation; substantial evidenced gaps |
| 5–6 | Plausible or partially supported; important gaps remain |
| 7–8 | Strong support appropriate to the product's current stage |
| 9–10 | Thorough relevant validation; still not a guarantee |

Use **Not assessed** when evidence is missing. Use **Not applicable** for irrelevant dimensions and say why. An early-stage idea is not automatically a zero. Do not invent scores for market demand, willingness to pay, accuracy, security, or profitability without evidence. A clearly missing release plan may justify a low readiness score, but not a conclusion that the business is unlawful or worthless.

Confidence is separate from the number:

- **High:** directly inspected, current, relevant evidence supports the rating.
- **Medium:** meaningful but partial evidence, with identified limitations.
- **Low:** preliminary judgment or small/unrepresentative samples.

## Dimensions to consider

Cover relevant dimensions, combining closely related ones for readability. Add domain-specific ones when material; “every aspect” does not require rating irrelevant categories or inventing evidence.

| Dimension | Evidence or decision that changes the score |
|---|---|
| Problem importance and user value | Frequency, severity, and observed improvement over the current process |
| Customer focus | Identifiable users, payer, context, geography, and access to them |
| Demand | Actual trials, repeat use, commitments, and purchases; not compliments alone |
| Differentiation | Verified alternatives and a benefit users can demonstrate |
| Acquisition | Reachable channels, conversion observations, cost and effort |
| Pricing and willingness to pay | Specific offer tested with relevant customers |
| Revenue-model fit | Payment trigger, repeat need, search/event cycles, retention or renewal |
| Unit economics and sustainability | Revenue less variable costs, fixed costs, support, acquisition, and relevant labor |
| Technical feasibility | Dependencies, required quality, team capability, and prototypes |
| First-version scope | A useful workflow within the user's resources; consequences of broad coverage |
| Data availability and quality | Rights, coverage, freshness, completeness, provenance, and replacement options |
| Reliability and output accuracy | Representative evaluation, failure handling, uncertainty, and expert review when relevant |
| Usability and accessibility | Observed task completion, usability issues, and accessibility checks |
| Security and privacy | Actual data flows, controls, retention, vendors, and relevant verification |
| Ownership, permissions, and credit readiness | Materials inventory, evidence, license obligations, contributor rights, and notices |
| Commercial/sector readiness | Actual marketing, contracts, billing, professional boundaries, and applicable jurisdictions |
| Maintenance and portability | Update process, documentation, backups, exports, staffing, and supplier dependence |

Use a compact table such as:

| Dimension | Score | Confidence | Evidence and next improvement |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Specific dimension | Number or status | High/medium/low | Brief basis; the action that would improve it |

Keep lengthy supporting detail outside the table. Explain score changes when a later report updates the same idea. Do not improve a score simply because the user prefers the idea or changes the wording of a claim.

By default give a verbal overall recommendation rather than a numeric average: **proceed to validation**, **proceed with conditions**, **revise a specified part**, or **pause the affected launch decision**. If an overall number is requested, label its nature, show weights or judgment method and coverage, and retain critical issues separately. No score is a success probability, legal clearance, or professional certification.

## Final report composition

Lead with the recommendation and the decisions driving it. A useful order is:

1. **Confirmed brief and assumptions:** current problem, user, payer, location, scope, price if chosen, resources, and date. Identify estimates and user-reported claims.
2. **Scorecard:** relevant dimensions, evidence, confidence, and improvements.
3. **Product and validation plan:** first useful workflow, alternatives, why someone would choose it, realistic experiment, and criteria agreed before measurement. Do not invent market-size figures or treat a small pilot as conclusive.
4. **Sources and rights:** materials actually known, obligations, required versus voluntary credits, unresolved evidence, and practical replacements. Summarize a long register and provide detail if needed.
5. **Delivery and economics:** scoped estimates, assumptions, uncertainty, costs, model diagram, and scenario calculations. Use the estimates reference.
6. **Action plan:** sequence work by value of information and dependencies, not by a generic development checklist.
7. **Release conditions and open decisions:** tie each unresolved issue to the specific activity it affects. A finalized report can still contain unknowns; it is not a certification that the product is ready to launch.

End the completed report with the optional PDF slide-deck offer described in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md#5-offer-optional-pdf-slides), unless the user already requested or declined it.

Actions should be concrete enough to delegate:

| Priority | Action | Owner | Effort | Depends on | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First/next/before release | Observable task | User/developer/domain reviewer, not an invented named person | Stated range or unknown | Relevant prior decision | Inspectable result or measured outcome |

Separate tasks the assistant can perform from interviews, provider decisions, professional review, and purchases the user or third parties must handle. Suggest interviews or outreach without sending messages unless authorized.

## Decision quality

- A licensing concern should lead to verifying permission, obtaining rights, choosing an alternative, or creating original material—not a wall of warnings.
- A cost problem should lead to scope, price, usage-limit, supplier, or delivery alternatives.
- A broad scope should receive an honest full-scope estimate and an optional staged approach, not be silently narrowed.
- An unsafe or unreliable output should lead to a product behavior such as asking for missing evidence, showing uncertainty, or withholding a conclusion, with a test for that behavior.
- Present professional-review questions precisely: the actual activity, relevant evidence/rule, uncertainty, and the decision needed. Do not prescribe a full legal audit for every small prototype.
